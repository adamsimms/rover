# Corpus governance

How the language fragments Fielding works from are gathered and changed. Draft; the protocol itself is written with the people it concerns.

## Principles

1. **Permission first.** Nothing enters the corpus without permission from whoever holds it: an archive, a publisher, a family member. No permission or archive partnership exists yet (OD-003; [#19](https://github.com/adamsimms/rover/issues/19) to [#21](https://github.com/adamsimms/rover/issues/21)). Applications and public text must not imply one.
2. **Not changed alone.** The artist does not add to, remove from or reweight the corpus alone. A consent-and-data protocol sets who may revise it, how, when and why.
3. **Direction belongs with Newfoundlanders.** The protocol is drafted with Newfoundlanders, the people who hold the dialect, not for them. Living contributors (including family oral history) go through the separate contributor consent process ([#22](https://github.com/adamsimms/rover/issues/22)).
4. **Restrict, don't train.** The fragments limit a capable model by lookup and citation. They are not training data (see [conceptual core](../concept/conceptual-core.md)).
5. **Host places are not corpus.** At residencies away from Newfoundland, local speech never enters the corpus and the place is not the work's cultural subject (see the [guest protocol](guest-protocol-fielding.md)).

## Sources named so far

The Dictionary of Newfoundland English, published resettlement records, and family oral history. Research leads: [corpus-research.md](../model/corpus-research.md).

## Open
