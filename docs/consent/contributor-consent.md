# Contributor consent

How living contributors' material is collected, used, stored, and, if requested, removed. Modeled on Tri-Council research-ethics practice, adapted for an independent arts context. Working document — reviewed with the first actual participant before being treated as final.

Covers all living contributors: family, researchers, or anyone whose words, stories, or identifications become part of the project.

## Two categories of contribution

**Category A — Object, place, and vocabulary identification.** A participant confirms names for objects, places, or terms. Treated as factual and generalizable — not primarily about the individual, often independently verifiable. Lighter protocol (Section 2).

**Category B — Interview, story, and personal account.** A participant shares a personal story, memory, or perspective in their own words (audio, video, or transcribed), following the Portelli-based methodology already central to this practice — the same standard used in *Mug Up*, where the participant held real, ongoing authority over how her voice and memory were represented. Substantially higher stakes (Sections 3–6).

## Category A protocol

- Contribution can be given anonymously or attributed, participant's choice at the time.
- No recording required — a written note or brief confirmation is sufficient.
- Participants are told before contributing: what it will be used for and that it may appear as part of a shared project vocabulary.
- Subject to full withdrawal procedures (Section 6) on request.

## Category B protocol — informed consent

Before any recording:

- A plain-language explanation of the project is given verbally and in writing: what the rover and AI system are, what the material will be used for, who will see it, and in what contexts.
- The participant is given a real, active choice — not a passive or assumed agreement. This applies fully to family members.
- Written consent is obtained specifying: what is being collected; how it may be used; how it will be attributed; that participation is voluntary, can be limited in scope, and can be withdrawn per Section 6; and contact details for withdrawal requests.

## Participant authority over representation

Following the *Mug Up* precedent:

- Before any AI-generated output derived from a participant's contribution is shown publicly, **the participant is shown that specific output and given the opportunity to review it.**
- Factual inaccuracies or misrepresentations are corrected to reflect the participant's own account — treated as editorial correction, not debate.
- If a participant objects on grounds of potential harm, the output is removed rather than edited.
- This review happens **each time** a new output from that participant's material is prepared for public use — not a one-time sign-off.

## Data storage

- Raw material stored securely, accessible only to the artist, not shared with third parties beyond what's needed for the AI training pipeline.
- No participant material submitted to public, consumer-facing AI tools that may retain submitted data.
- Retained only for the project's duration unless the participant explicitly agrees to longer-term retention.

## Withdrawal

**Always possible, fully and reliably:**
- Immediate removal of raw material from the active archive.
- Removal or editing of any AI-generated output derived from that material.
- Exclusion from all future public use, exhibition, or publication.

**Requires a named additional step, disclosed upfront:**
- Fully removing a participant's *influence* from an already-trained model is a genuinely unresolved technical challenge ("machine unlearning") — deleting source material does not undo its effect on a model already trained on it. At this project's scale, the honest solution is **full retraining without that participant's material**. More resource-intensive than deletion, but the only method that can be fully trusted. Participants are told this plainly at the consent stage.

## Scaling

- Protocol applies identically to all participants, whether among the first or joining later.
- Future participants may be shown, if they wish, how earlier participants' material has been used (in general terms) before deciding whether to join.
- Any revision to this protocol is communicated to current participants, who may have consent re-confirmed under revised terms or withdraw.

## Note on invitee involvement and model training

Invitee involvement and model training assistance are not parallel workstreams. Invitees train and correct the model directly during the deployment phase — their participation IS the training assistance. This distinction matters for consent: participants are consenting to active model shaping, not observation alongside a separate technical process.
