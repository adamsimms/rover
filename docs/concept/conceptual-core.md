# Conceptual core

*Uncertain Field Notes* asks how you represent a place that no longer exists. Rover is a solar- and cellular-powered device for Pinchard's Island, a resettled outport off the northeast coast of Newfoundland (Ktaqmkuk). It carries a camera and sensing hardware, and a language model that writes field notes about what the device observes.

The conceptual center is not the chassis. It is **linguistic failure made visible**: a capable model held to fragmentary language and historical materials (pieces that stand in for broken or incomplete cultural transmission) rather than a fluent, inherited relation to the place. The fragments restrict the model; they do not train it. It looks words up, cites them, and works only with what survives. The notes accumulate as a living, self-revising record that can contradict earlier entries, misname what it sees, and ask questions instead of claiming finished description. The artwork is the gap between the photograph and the uncertain note.

The rover is on the cellular network. It can reverse-geocode a point and fetch a weather record the way any networked machine can. That data comes from elsewhere (a gazetteer, a forecast, a station that is not the shore). It is not the same as inheriting the words for the place. When the radio is down, it falls back to looking, and marks the guess the way a field note does when the instruments are out. A header that can name the island and the wind, next to a note still hunting for a word, is part of the pressure: limited by language, not starved of information.

That framing is why "getting it right" as pure engineering is not enough on its own. Engineering still matters (survival through winter, power, safety), but the artwork's pressure is on incomplete speech and incomplete belonging.

**Against extraction:** industrial AI gets its fluency by ingesting everything it can reach, including books cut apart to be scanned. Rover goes the other way: a small, fragmentary body of language that is held and safeguarded rather than possessed, and a machine that shows where the words run out.

**Resettlement mechanism:** the Government of Newfoundland and Labrador's own policy never legally prohibited return — property owners retain title and can access their properties at their own cost and risk. What resettlement actually withdrew was every service that makes ordinary life possible: garbage collection, water and sewer, street lighting, road maintenance, ferry service, schools, health services, electricity. No villain, no law to point to; infrastructure quietly removed. That is a sharper, colder version of the permanence argument than a prohibition would be.

**Resident, not proxy:** one framing inverts the rover from the artist's proxy — an instrument controlled, reaching toward the island from Montreal — to something that *lives there*: a presence that exists on the island whether or not anyone connects to it that week, with days and conditions the artist never witnesses. The artist becomes the visitor to it, not the other way around. In 1953 fewer than 200 remaining residents moved to Newtown; a permanently-resident rover repopulates the island seven decades later — just not with a person.

**Productive mismatch:** the Newfoundland fragments are an epistemic constraint, not a geographic ceiling. When Fielding looks at a different landscape with a partial NL tongue, the productive failure is mismatch: wrong metaphor, category error, resettlement affect bleeding onto another politics of emptiness, self-revision when the wrong word is noticed. Residencies enlarge that comparative pressure; they do not require a bigger training world. A fluent "isolation everywhere" corpus would sand the failure into competent eco-reportage.

**IP AI literature:** adjacent Indigenous Protocol / AI literature (Making Kin, the IP AI Position Paper, Abundant Intelligences, Reworlding) is useful to this project as *method* — locality, material and extended stack, refusal, rights before scraping, no terra nullius. It is not the project's cosmology, corpus, or branding ancestry. The concept stays self-owned (linguistic failure, fragmentary notes, guest instrument); cite quietly with a boundary sentence if funders ask. Not a contrast manifesto that uses Indigenous fluency as foil.

**Climate as condition:** climate and ecology belong in this work as condition and politics of place — solar stress, coastal weather, governed remoteness — not as a curriculum the model is trained to deliver. The strongest fit is a machine stranded in a climate whose language cannot keep up with what the sensors see. Do not invent climate-science necessity to match a residency host's sustainability framing; belonging, displacement, and emptied purpose are sharper.

**Split site:** testing and deployment are conceptually distinct. Testing needs a reachable, comparable coastal site — it does not need inherited meaning. Deployment needs the real claim. Little Bay Islands serves Stage 1 and 2 (accessible, with a living parallel: Mike and Georgina Parsons, who stayed by choice, off-grid and solar-powered, since 2019); Pinchard's Island is Stage 3, the site the work is actually about.

**Practice lineage:** the rover extends a prior practice of solar-powered, LTE-connected, long-duration outdoor camera work — Cloudberry (solar camera, year-long landscape documentation) and Adrift (weather-data virtual recreation). The move from fixed camera to mobile device is a physical extension of that practice, not a departure from it.

**Thematic axes from naming research:** two threads run through the vocabulary and framing of the project. The first is the insider/outsider identity binary — Bayman/Townie/Livyer, the tension between belonging and passing through — which the rover literalizes as a machine from away that cannot inherit the place. The second is disguise and doubling — Janny (the costumed mummerer), Fetch (an Irish-NL folklore double of a living person sent somewhere they cannot be) — which frames Fielding as a proxy standing in for a presence that is not there.

**Permissions:** the sources named for the corpus (the Dictionary of Newfoundland English, published resettlement records, family oral history) are not cleared for this use, and no archive partnership exists yet (OD-003). Public docs describe intent; this repo includes no corpus text. How the corpus is assembled and changed is governed by the [corpus governance](../consent/corpus-governance.md) page.

**Naming:** the work is titled *Uncertain Field Notes*. The device is Fielding; a longer literary justification for that name is withheld until permission is confirmed ([#23](https://github.com/adamsimms/rover/issues/23)), and applications may hold the name out and say *rover*, *device* or *machine* (not *instrument*).
