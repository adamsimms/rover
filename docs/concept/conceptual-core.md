# Conceptual core

*Uncertain Field Notes* asks how you represent a place that no longer exists. Rover is a solar- and cellular-powered device for Pinchard's Island, a resettled outport off the northeast coast of Newfoundland (Ktaqmkuk). It carries a camera and sensing hardware, and a language model that writes field notes about what the device observes.

The conceptual center is not the chassis. It is **linguistic failure made visible**: a capable model held to fragmentary language and historical materials (pieces that stand in for broken or incomplete cultural transmission) rather than a fluent, inherited relation to the place. The fragments restrict the model; they do not train it. It looks words up, cites them, and works only with what survives. The notes accumulate as a living, self-revising record that can contradict earlier entries, misname what it sees, and ask questions instead of claiming finished description. The artwork is the gap between the photograph and the uncertain note.

The rover is on the cellular network. It can reverse-geocode a point and fetch a weather record the way any networked machine can. That data comes from elsewhere (a gazetteer, a forecast, a station that is not the shore). It is not the same as inheriting the words for the place. When the radio is down, it falls back to looking, and marks the guess the way a field note does when the instruments are out. A header that can name the island and the wind, next to a note still hunting for a word, is part of the pressure: limited by language, not starved of information.

That framing is why "getting it right" as pure engineering is not enough on its own. Engineering still matters (survival through winter, power, safety), but the artwork's pressure is on incomplete speech and incomplete belonging.

**Against extraction:** industrial AI gets its fluency by ingesting everything it can reach, including books cut apart to be scanned. Rover goes the other way: a small, fragmentary body of language that is held and safeguarded rather than possessed, and a machine that shows where the words run out.

**Permissions:** the sources named for the corpus (the Dictionary of Newfoundland English, published resettlement records, family oral history) are not cleared for this use, and no archive partnership exists yet (OD-003). Public docs describe intent; this repo includes no corpus text. How the corpus is assembled and changed is governed by the [corpus governance](../consent/corpus-governance.md) page.

**Naming:** the work is titled *Uncertain Field Notes*. The device is Fielding; a longer literary justification for that name is withheld until permission is confirmed ([#23](https://github.com/adamsimms/rover/issues/23)), and applications may hold the name out and say *rover*, *device* or *machine* (not *instrument*).
