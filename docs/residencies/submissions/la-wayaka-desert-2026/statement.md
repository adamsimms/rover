I am applying to Desert 23°S to do foundational work for a multi-year autonomous, off-grid, networked rover.

My practice combines photography, transmission, and microelectronics to explore place, language, and belonging. I am currently building two connected parts of the larger rover project: a camera system and an image-to-text field-note process using a generative language model. I am building this to test what happens when photographs meet fragmented cultural memory and are translated, or mistranslated, into language. The language model is constrained by incomplete place-memory traces, including fragments of dialect, oral history, and archival material from Newfoundland, an island on Canada’s North Atlantic coast. This residency arrives at the right moment, when conceptual clarity and field testing matter most.

I want to work in Atacama because its remoteness and dryness create the conditions I need for sustained attention and careful testing. During the residency, I would refine the project’s conceptual and visual language, how the rover thinks and sees, and prototype the camera system within the real constraints of site, power, and weather.

La Wayaka’s format fits how I work. Independent time, field visits, and sessions with local collaborators would allow me to produce early outcomes and build a stronger method for what comes next.
