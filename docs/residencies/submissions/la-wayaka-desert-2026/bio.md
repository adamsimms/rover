Adam Simms is a Canadian media artist based in Montreal. He creates installations that integrate photography, video, live data, and microelectronics. Much of his work transmits landscape, dialect, and vernacular material between remote sites and unexpected locations, with a focus on belonging, displacement, and intangible culture.

His practice begins from Newfoundland, an island on Canada’s North Atlantic coast, and from a position both inside and outside that history. Through his family’s history of resettlement, Adam draws parallels to broader experiences of displacement, asking which aspects of culture are portable and which remain rooted in place.

Projects including Cloudberry, Cabin, Adrift, and Driftwood use remote cameras, transmission systems, and sensor-based forms to explore partial presence, distance, and memory. Through these works, he develops image-making systems that treat environment, infrastructure, and cultural memory as interconnected material.

His work has been shown at the Leonard & Bina Ellen Art Gallery and Occurrence, and published in Ciel Variable, Riddle Fence, and Photographer’s FORUM. He holds a BFA and MFA from Concordia University and has taught there as an Artist in Residence Professor. Alongside his studio practice, he works in design and software, which informs how he builds artistic systems for sensing, transmission, and image-making.
