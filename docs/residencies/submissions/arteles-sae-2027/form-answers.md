## Who are you? Short introduction / background *

**Status:** Locked 2026-07-28 · submitting

I am a quiet, curious person, easy in silence, and most myself in a studio: making, looking, thinking until something finds its shape. I'm a media artist based in Montreal who works with photography, video, and microelectronics, often moving landscape and vernacular material between remote locations and cities. Newfoundland is home, though years away have also made me an outsider to it. My connection to Pinchard's Island, an empty island off Newfoundland, keeps returning in the work: belonging, displacement, and what of a place can still be held when the people are gone.

Projects like *Cloudberry*, *Cabin*, and *Driftwood* brought coastal Newfoundland into other rooms through remote cameras, live feeds, and sensor data. That line of work led to what I am building now: an off-grid, networked field rover.

I hold a BFA in Design and an MFA in Studio Arts (Photography) from Concordia University.

## What makes you apply / what would you focus on? *

I'm applying because of how this program is built: silent days, limited distraction, and no pressure to leave with a finished project. That matches the kind of attention my current work needs. I want time to look carefully, write, and test without rushing toward an outcome, and I want that among other people who came for the same reason.

I'm in the early stages of a multi-year project: an autonomous, off-grid field rover built for Pinchard's Island, an empty remote island off the coast of Newfoundland that was once home to my grandmother. The rover pairs a camera with a language model that discovers the island by taking photographs and writing observational field notes. The project is rooted in a question that has followed my work for years: how to represent a place that no longer exists. And what remains of intangible culture once the community is gone and the language that held it is thinning. The field notes are limited by Newfoundland dialect and related fragments, so image and text struggle to line up. The rover will misname, contradict itself, and open ways of seeing the place that fluency would close. Those gaps are part of the work.

During this residency I would focus on building and testing that image-to-note pairing. Research and writing in the mornings; camera tests and model experiments against the winter landscape; walking when I need to clear my head. I hope to leave with a clearer framing of the project and a working set of camera-to-note tests. No hardware fabrication. Quiet contact with the group is part of what I'm here for, not an afterthought.
