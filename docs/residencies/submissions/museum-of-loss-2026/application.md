## Motivation (submitted, 254 words)

My work returns to a single question: how do you represent a place that no longer exists? It grows from my family's resettlement from a small Newfoundland island, a community that quietly fell off the map, and from the position I now occupy, one generation removed, reconstructing that place from fragments: an archive, a dialect dictionary, inherited stories.

I am applying for Taking Time / Prendendo Tempo to develop the research-and-development phase of a new work built around this question: a solar- and cellular-powered imaging device carrying a language model trained on those fragments, writing field notes about what it observes. The notes are meant to stay uncertain. They misname, they guess, they admit where words have been lost, so that incomplete cultural transmission becomes visible rather than smoothed over.

This phase is not about producing a finished object. It is where the technical and conceptual threads have to be thought through together: how the device observes, how the model writes from what remains, and how honestly its failures can be held as part of the record rather than hidden. That asks for sustained, unhurried attention, where reading, writing, testing and reflection inform one another daily. It is hard to find inside ordinary routines.

The residency's emphasis on unhurried, attentive work matches how this must be made. I would use the time to refine the relationship between image and generated text, to test the project's assumptions against real writing, and to arrive at a firm direction for the fabrication and deployment stages that follow.

## Relevance (submitted, 271 words)

I want to work in Collemacchia because it lives, from the other side, the condition my practice is about. My work concerns places thinned by out-migration and the incomplete belonging that follows. Collemacchia has been shaped by that same depopulation, yet people keep returning and hold it open rather than letting it go quiet. Being in a place that carries this history differently from the one I know would give me a vantage I cannot reach from home, and it is why this residency, specifically, matters to my work.

What I would most like to develop here is the slower, interdisciplinary side of the project: uninterrupted time to move between reading, writing, testing and reflection, without the fragmentation of ordinary routines. The on-site library's strengths in art writing, publishing and walking, with informal mentoring and honest feedback, would help me push the writing at the centre of the work, its field-note voice, further than I can alone. Being among residents from other disciplines is part of what I am seeking, not incidental to it.

The work is heading toward field notes that pair what a camera sees with written text, gathered over time into a book or an evolving glossary, so a place grounded in writing, publishing and the image sits close to what I am making. Walking the landscape is itself part of how the project works. In return I would bring an unusual mix for a studio setting: photography, writing and machine made language drawn together, along with care for shared living and studio space and a real willingness to share process and thinking without forcing a finished result.
